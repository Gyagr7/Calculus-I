<title>Pre-Assignment: Pancake Paints the Barn</title>

<p>
  Pancake the alpaca has been banned from racing (long story), so the
  farmer gave her a new job: painting the side of the barn. She pushes a
  paint roller from left to right. The top edge of the paint follows a
  curve <m>y = f(t)</m>.
</p>

<p>
  When Pancake reaches position <m>x</m>, the painted area behind her is
  <m>A(x)</m>. Before Tuesday, let's figure out how fast that painted
  area grows. About 10 minutes. A wrong guess is completely fine.
</p>

<p>
  <alert>Question 1.</alert> On the first wall, the paint is a steady
  <m>3</m> meters tall, so <m>f(t) = 3</m>. The painted region is a
  rectangle. When Pancake reaches <m>x = 4</m>, how many square meters
  has she painted?
</p>

<answer>
  <label>Painted area at x = 4</label>
  <award>12</award>
</answer>

<p>
  In general, on this wall <m>A(x) = 3x</m>. Every meter Pancake moves
  adds <m>3</m> more square meters. Keep that <m>3</m> in mind.
</p>

<p>
  On the second wall, the paint gets taller as Pancake goes:
  <m>f(t) = 2t</m>. Now the painted region is a triangle. Drag the slider
  to move Pancake and watch the painted area change.
</p>

<p>Pancake's position: <slider name="x" from="0" to="4" step="0.5" initialValue="2" /></p>

<graph xmin="-0.5" xmax="4.5" ymin="-0.5" ymax="9">
  <function>2x</function>
  <polygon vertices="(0,0) ($x,0) ($x,2$x)" />
  <point>($x, 0)</point>
</graph>

<p>
  At <m>x =</m> <number>$x</number>, the painted area is
  <number>$x^2</number> square meters.
</p>

<p>
  <alert>Question 2.</alert> Use the triangle, or use the numbers from the
  slider. Which formula gives the painted area <m>A(x)</m> on the second
  wall?
</p>

<answer>
  <label>Which formula gives A(x)?</label>
  <choice credit="1"><m>A(x) = x^2</m></choice>
  <choice credit="0"><m>A(x) = 2x</m></choice>
  <choice credit="0"><m>A(x) = 2x^2</m></choice>
  <choice credit="0"><m>A(x) = x^2/2</m></choice>
</answer>

<p>
  <alert>Question 3.</alert> How fast is the painted area growing? Take the
  derivative of the area function. If <m>A(x) = x^2</m>, what is
  <m>A'(x)</m>?
</p>

<answer>
  <label>The derivative of the area function</label>
  <award>2x</award>
</answer>

<p>
  Now look back. On the first wall, the paint height was <m>3</m> and the
  area grew at rate <m>3</m>. On the second wall, the paint height was
  <m>2x</m> and the area grew at rate <m>2x</m>. Suspicious...
</p>

<p>
  <alert>Question 4.</alert> Make a guess. What do you think is always
  true about the painted area <m>A(x)</m> and the paint height
  <m>f(x)</m>?
</p>

<answer>
  <label>Your conjecture</label>
  <choice credit="1"><m>A'(x) = f(x)</m>: the area grows exactly as fast as the paint is tall</choice>
  <choice credit="0"><m>A(x) = f(x)</m>: the area equals the height</choice>
  <choice credit="0"><m>A'(x) = A(x)</m>: the area grows as fast as the area itself</choice>
  <choice credit="0">There is no pattern, it was a coincidence</choice>
</answer>

<p>
  Remember the car with speed <m>v(t) = 3t^2</m>? You found that it
  traveled <m>8</m> meters by running a derivative backward, and
  <m>8</m> again by adding up rectangles. Your answer to Question 4 is the
  reason those matched. On Tuesday we find out why it has to be true.
</p>
